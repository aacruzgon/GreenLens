Follow one scan. The app signs in with Clerk and sends the photo to our Rust API, which keeps it private in RustFS and queues jobs in SurrealDB. The two GPU workers at home pull those jobs over outbound HTTPS, so the rig has no open ports, and post their results back. Claude writes the plan, and live progress streams to the phone the whole way. If no worker claims a vision job within 15 seconds, Claude takes it.

Here are the models on one scan. Faces are blurred on upload, and the upload fails closed. The perception worker finds the leaves, checks there is a plant, draws masks, names the species and runs our disease head: 6 photos and 40 leaf crops in 1.2 seconds on one 8 GB card. The vision-language model adds its findings. Then the API checks the candidates against curated disease profiles. It confirms one only when it leads by a margin; otherwise it says "not sure yet" and asks you a question. No single model makes the call.

Accuracy is for the disease head alone. End-to-end diagnosis accuracy is not measured yet.

What we measured. Our disease head puts the true problem in its top three on 93% of garden-like photos, against 62% for guessing, and 84% on a held-out test split we scored once. That's on 267 labelled cases, and perception takes 1.2 seconds. And what we built in: open-licensed models, faces blurred and metadata stripped before storage, no open ports at home, and Clerk sign-in with MFA.
