## Model notes (as first drafted)

Late blight is caused by a water mould (Phytophthora infestans), not a true fungus. 'fungal' is the closest kind available. The excerpts do not say how common late blight is. They only call it potentially serious, so 'occasional' is a guess for the reviewer to check. The excerpts do not say which leaves (lower or upper) show spots first. 'lower_leaves' for the leaf-spot indicators is a placeholder. The weather is stated as 'cool, wet weather' and also as daytime highs in the upper 70s °F (about 24 to 26 °C) with high humidity. No humidity percentage or hours are given. No sizes or counts are given, so there are no measures. No excerpt calls a symptom characteristic, so all weights are 2. Excerpt 17 says a greasy, water-soaked area on the blossom end of green fruit, caused by an unnamed disease, is very similar to late blight's fruit symptom. It also mentions buckeye rot. Neither is in the comparison list, so no looks_like entry was made. Excerpt 19 says buckeye rot fruit lesions stay firm and smooth, while late blight lesions become rough and slightly sunken at the edges. This is used as a fruit indicator. The excerpts compare late blight with no listed problem. The fungicide step reuses fungicide_early_blight because its text matches the excerpt (severe disease: chlorothalonil, copper or mancozeb, follow the label). The excerpt rates chlorothalonil 'very good' and copper and mancozeb 'good'. The excerpts do not say these fungicides only protect before infection. space_plants_apart is reused for 'allow extra room between the plants'. Tables 1 and 3 (products and resistant varieties) are referred to but not given.
