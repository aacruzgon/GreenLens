## Results

Both runs passed: every call decoded and validated with the worker's code, with each candidate's indicators answered, on the first attempt (no retry, no answer cut at its token budget).

| Call | Fake server (FP8 recordings) | Qwen3-VL-2B Q8_0, llama.cpp b11528, CPU |
|---|---|---|
| Decode and validate | ✅ 7 of 7 | ✅ 7 of 7 |
| Tomato findings | tomato (0.85); top `aphid`, 2 boxes, 2 soil readings | tomato (0.85); top `tomato_hornworm`, 3 boxes, 8 soil readings; 164 s |
| Tomato indicators (yes, no, unsure) | 0, 8, 1 | 6, 2, 1; 89 s |
| Tomato verify | 0, 8, 1 | 7, 1, 1; 89 s |
| Corn findings | corn (0.85); top `fall_armyworm`, 4 boxes, 0 soil readings | corn (0.85); top `ladybug_adult`, 2 boxes, 0 soil readings; 92 s |
| Corn indicators | 3, 5, 1 | 9, 0, 0; 104 s |
| Squeeze and jar tests | sticky ribbon; clayey | crumbles; sandy; 35 s and 39 s |
| Whole run | 38 s | 650 s |

- **The fake server logs `text drift` on the three indicator calls.** `indicators@3` (#137) changed the `reason` line ("under 40 characters" to "at most 60 characters") after the committed recordings, so the server replays the `indicators@2` answer. It still validates, but the recordings are one prompt version behind: re-record them on the rig (`fake_vlm.py --record … --upstream …`).
- **Qwen3-VL-2B leans to `yes`** (22 of 27 indicator answers, all 9 on the corn) and names a beneficial insect (`ladybug_adult`) on the corn. It reads the soil shot (8 readings) and leaves the corn's soil null, as `findings@2` intends. As section 5 says, it tests the wiring, not accuracy.
- **Speed:** about 33 prompt tokens per second and 11–13 decoded tokens per second on this CPU; the three-image tomato findings call sent 4,187 prompt tokens (100 s of prompt processing).
